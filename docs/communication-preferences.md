# Communication preferences

Open **HITL review → Communication preferences** to record an opt-out, bounced address, complaint or manual hold, including a received time and optional internal note. The same controls appear in **Discovery → account → Contacts** with its domain prefilled. Controls are free and available with zero accounts or credits. Requests are user-reported, not provider-verified consent.

Each business keeps its own preferences. An account hold matches the canonical domain and its subdomains, blocks targeted vendor/company/contact/person processing and all outreach for matching accounts, and removes them from the shortlist. An email or LinkedIn hold matches that channel across the business’s accounts; it leaves the other channel available. Email recipient domains also respect account holds. Public company research, fit/graph facts, historical outcomes and dispatched receipts remain available. This is not deletion or a universal ban on public intelligence gathering.

Matching email addresses ignores case; one plain address or a single display-name recipient is accepted for outreach. LinkedIn targets require an HTTPS `/in/` profile on linkedin.com; matching ignores case, tracking parameters, fragments and trailing slashes, and decodes safe path characters. Format validation does not establish profile ownership. Unparseable nonempty recipients cannot be approved or dispatched. Empty recipients may still receive copy approval, but cannot record a dry run.

New holds clear affected editable recipients and exact-message approvals. Contact selection, drafting, approval and dry runs recheck the server policy. Model, MX, vendor and person source work pin the business preference context: any preference version change during work, even a hold followed by release, invalidates its result. Local reservations are refunded once. External processing or charges already incurred are not cancelled. Vendor search/verification records with an older preference context require fresh research. A held LinkedIn profile is not copied into a newly selected email contact.

Releasing a hold requires the current record version, a reason, a decision note and acknowledgement. Release does not restore recipients, shortlist selections, approvals or provider processing permission. Provider restrictions are separate and cannot be removed with these controls. Stable request IDs prevent retry duplication and old hold/release replays from overriding later decisions. Versioned decisions are recorded in local SQLite and survive campaign reset and restart.

Email/profile targets are stored as business-scoped SHA-256 matching keys with masked display labels; account domains remain visible. Hashing is not encryption or erasure. Internal decision notes and records remain outside model payloads, provider requests and native graph projection. They are visible to holders of this project’s shared access token; this prototype does not implement individual user identities or regulated consent management. No live email or LinkedIn delivery is enabled. Hunter remains disabled without project-specific configuration.

Validation: normalization/bypass cases; cross-channel and cross-business matching; preemptive domains; exact approval invalidation and immutable receipts; request replay and stale releases; provider restriction separation; in-flight MX/model/vendor/person races and local refunds; authenticated typed HTTP controls; campaign reset and SQLite reopen.

The complete suite has 228 passing tests, including 12 communication-control tests; the production build passes. Desktop QA exercised account hold/release, acknowledgement reset, channel-scoped holds, recipient/approval clearing, disabled dispatch and independent business state in an isolated synthetic workspace. No manual hold or synthetic contact was added to the main campaigns.
